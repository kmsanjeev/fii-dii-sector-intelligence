# VEDA-KNOW-TIMING-001 — Dasha and Transit Governance

## Dasha

The inspected BPHS material supports a layered but conditional use of Dasha:
the period lord's house placement, lordship, relationships, strength and
Antardasha context affect results. It explicitly gives Dasha-period outcomes
such as educational improvement, house/land, children, marriage, profession,
religious-mindedness and pilgrimage in different passages.

Governed conclusion: Dasha can be recorded as an activation window or
period-level condition when the relevant structural promise exists. It cannot
be converted into a universal first-employment, degree-completion, purchase,
relocation or initiation detector without an event-specific source contract.

## Transit

Phaladeepika Chapter 16 verse 35 gives a narrow house-success transit condition
involving conjunction of the Lagna lord and the relevant house lord, subject to
strength. Chapter 17 provides explicit Saturn/Jupiter/Sun/Moon transit rules in
a death-timing context. These passages prove that some classical transit
doctrine exists; they do not authorize a generic “Jupiter/Saturn transit
triggers every event” recipe.

## Promise → Dasha → Transit

The hierarchy is:

`STRUCTURAL PROMISE → DASHA/ANTARDASHA ACTIVATION → TRANSIT REFINEMENT`

Classification: `PLATFORM_SYNTHESIS`, informed by classical components. No
single inspected passage was found that defines this entire cross-domain
architecture as VEDA uses it. A transit cannot create a result where the
structural promise and period activation are absent.

## Precision

Classical passages support broad Dasha periods, sub-periods, and in selected
methods transit windows. They do not support universal exact-date precision.
Year- or period-level outcomes must not be scored as exact-day predictions.

## Obstruction and termination

Adverse Dasha placement, weakness, inimical relationships and 6th/8th/12th
contexts are source-backed as adverse or obstructive conditions in selected
passages. They are not universal denial rules. Termination is not inferred from
delay alone; event-family-specific doctrine is required.
